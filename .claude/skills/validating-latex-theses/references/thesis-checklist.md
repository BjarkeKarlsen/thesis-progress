# Thesis Validation Checklist

## Project and Build

- Root document identified.
- Include graph resolved.
- Reading order established.
- Bibliography backend identified.
- Project compiles.
- Missing resources checked.
- Build warnings collected.
- Generated and external files excluded appropriately.

## Citations

- Citation keys resolve.
- Bibliography keys are unique.
- Central technical claims are traceable.
- Citation scope is clear.
- Narrative and parenthetical citations are grammatical.
- Direct quotations have required locators.
- Original and secondary sources are distinguished where relevant.
- Literature summaries synthesize sources.

## Internal References

- Labels are unique.
- References resolve.
- Object types are clear.
- Current-document numbers are not hard-coded unnecessarily.
- Figures and tables are introduced.
- Central objects are interpreted.
- Label conventions are coherent.

## Definitions and Notation

- Acronyms are expanded.
- Specialized terms are defined before substantive use.
- Symbols are defined.
- Sets and functions have adequate meaning.
- Graph roles are distinguished.
- Global state and local observation are distinguished.
- Agent sets and action spaces are not conflated.
- Notation table matches body text.
- Adopted and original definitions are distinguished.

## Structure

- Motivation is specific.
- Problem statement is precise.
- Research gap is supported.
- Aim and research questions are explicit.
- Contributions are identifiable.
- Method maps to research questions.
- Experiments have analytical purposes.
- Metrics match the evaluated claims.
- Results are interpreted.
- Limitations are discussed.
- Conclusion answers the questions.

## Writing

- Claims are specific.
- Comparisons identify baselines and metrics.
- Certainty matches evidence.
- Paragraphs have coherent purposes.
- Transitions reflect actual logic.
- Repetition has a clear function.
- Terminology is stable.
- Authorial design reasoning is visible.
- Grammar issues do not obscure meaning.

## Rubric

- Every demand has an evidence location or missing status.
- Every criterion has an evidence map.
- Mandatory requirements are prioritized.
- Scores have justifications.
- Not-assessable criteria are not scored.
- Overall score reflects blockers and critical omissions.

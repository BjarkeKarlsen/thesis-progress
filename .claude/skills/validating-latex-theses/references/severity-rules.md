# Severity and Confidence Rules

## Severity Decision Order

Ask these questions in order:

1. Does the issue prevent compilation or assessment? Use Blocker.
2. Does it undermine the central argument, correctness, or traceability? Use Critical.
3. Does it materially weaken a chapter, research question, method, or result? Use Major.
4. Is it localized and correctable without changing the main argument? Use Minor.
5. Is it optional improvement rather than a demonstrated deficiency? Use Suggestion.

## Escalation Factors

Increase severity when:

- The issue affects a research question.
- The issue occurs repeatedly across chapters.
- The issue affects a central equation, definition, dataset, result, or claim.
- The rubric marks the requirement as mandatory or highly weighted.
- The issue creates a false or unsupported conclusion.
- The issue prevents reproducibility.

## De-escalation Factors

Decrease severity when:

- The issue is isolated.
- The intended meaning remains unambiguous.
- The project follows an explicit alternative convention.
- The issue occurs only in supplementary material.
- The finding depends strongly on subjective style preference.

## Confidence Rules

Use High confidence for:

- Compilation errors.
- Undefined identifiers confirmed by the build.
- Exact contradictions with explicit requirements.
- Duplicate labels.
- Directly observable terminology conflicts.

Use Medium confidence for:

- Likely unsupported claims.
- Citation-scope interpretations.
- Structural placement concerns.
- Definition-quality concerns.

Use Low confidence for:

- Voice-change observations.
- Possible formulaic style.
- Source-quality concerns without source inspection.
- Suggested reorganization where several structures are valid.

Low-confidence findings must use cautious language and should not determine severe penalties alone.

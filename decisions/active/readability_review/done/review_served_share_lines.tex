\section*{Part 1. What went wrong}

The fleet 16 replay with the served share came home on 17 November. Token Passing reads 1.00 under
every rule, as expected. Several learned runs read above 1 for the picks. A share above 1 is allowed
by design, since a fleet that clears an old queue finishes more than arrives. These runs are not
clearing a queue. Their queues grow fastest.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
  \toprule
  run (one seed) & tasks finished & pick share & orders waiting for stock at the end \\
  \midrule
  decentralised, fixed & 0 & 6.0 & 835 \\
  centralised, fixed & 81 & 0.98 & 498 \\
  section-based, congestion-aware & 549 & 0.81 & 205 \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The cause is when a pick becomes a task. An order line arrives, but its pick task is made only once
its stock is on a shelf. Until then the line waits as an order. A fleet whose put-aways fail gets
almost no stock onto the shelves, so almost no pick tasks are made. The check then divides by a
handful of released picks and reads high. The decentralised run above finished no task at all and
reads 6.0.

The backlog $B_T$ already counts the waiting orders. Only the check leaves them out. So the fix is to
count them in the check as well.

A second fault is in how the replay ran, not in the measure. It played each spec's own number of test
seeds. The fixed and demand specs of the section-based arm, the decentralised arm and the arm with
messages list 6, so 9 of the 16 runs played 6 seeds, not 20. The main test passed \texttt{--test-seeds
20}. The new replay will do the same.

\subsection*{The same fault in the throughput}

The throughput $\Lambda_T$ has the fault [A96] fixed in the check. It counts only the tasks released
in the window, so a fleet that fell behind gets no credit for the older tasks it finishes. The
replay rows hold both counts. Tasks done per step, means over the replayed seeds:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llrr}
  \toprule
  arm & rule & $\Lambda_T$ now & every task finished in the window \\
  \midrule
  centralised & fixed & 0.036 & 0.143 \\
  section-based & fixed & 0.147 & 0.186 \\
  decentralised & fixed & 0.000 & 0.030 \\
  decentralised & congestion-aware & 0.104 & 0.186 \\
  with messages & fixed & 0.013 & 0.113 \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The learned arms do far more work than $\Lambda_T$ shows, and the gaps between them shrink. The
centralised arm does four times as much. Token Passing keeps up, so both counts agree for it. The
proposal is that $\Lambda_T$ counts every task finished in the window, the set
$\mathcal{D}_T$ of the check, so the throughput and the served share count the same work. The
tasks per step in Appendix~L and the results review, the Regime~1 table among them, then change
with the replay.

\section*{Part 2. The text}

\subsection*{Problem Formulation, ``Keeping up'' (lines 1178 to 1183)}

The check counts every task the fleet finishes in the measured steps, also one released before them.
A fleet that fell behind earlier still works through its older tasks, and that work is what shows
whether the queue grows or holds. So the measure is the share of the arriving work the fleet serves.
With 900 tasks released in the measured steps and 600 tasks finished in them, the share is two thirds
and the queue grows by 300.

\new{A pick counts as released when its order arrives, also while its stock is not yet on a shelf.
The order is work the fleet owes, and the backlog already counts it. Left out, a fleet whose
put-aways fail would release almost no picks and seem to keep up. Such a fleet with 800 orders
waiting for stock and 10 picks finished serves about one pick in eighty.}

\subsection*{Problem Formulation, the formal part (lines 1193 to 1196)}

Formally, \old{with $\mathcal{C}_T$ the tasks completed within a horizon $T$, throughput is
$\Lambda_T = |\mathcal{C}_T| / T$.} \new{throughput is $\Lambda_T = |\mathcal{D}_T| / T$, with
$\mathcal{D}_T = \mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{pick}}_T \cup \mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{put}}_T$ the tasks finished in
the measured window, whenever they were released.}
Write $\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{pick}}_T$ for the picks released in the measured window
\new{, with one for each order line from the window that still waits for stock at the horizon,}
and $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{pick}}_T$ for the picks finished in it, whenever they were released, and
the same for put-aways. The served share of a kind is the second over the first \dots

Equation \texttt{eq:throughput} and $f_{\mathrm{up}} = 0.8$ stay as they are.

\subsection*{What changes in the code}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \texttt{served\_counts} adds to the released picks every order line released in the window
  that still misses units at $T$, once per line. The finished side does not change.
\item The throughput counts every task finished in the window, as \texttt{num\_finished\_in\_window}
  does already. \texttt{num\_completed\_tasks} keeps its old meaning under a new name, so old rows
  can still be read.
\item The rows keep \texttt{num\_released\_in\_window} and add the number of waiting lines from the
  window, so the two parts can be read apart.
\item A test, a run with no put-away done, must read a pick share near 0, not above 1.
\item Both replays (fleet 16 Regime 2 and Regime 1) and the fleet 8 replay run again with
  \texttt{--test-seeds 20}. About 15 hours for fleet 16 on the PC.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{What it changes, checked on the replay rows}

The rows already hold what both fixes need, so they were checked before any replay.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{The pick fix changes no keep-up verdict}, in any of the 12 cells. A run whose put-aways
  fail already fails the put-away part. The corrected pick share lands close to the put-away share
  (centralised fixed 0.54 against 0.51), because picks wait for the stock the put-aways bring. So
  this fix only corrects the number reported, and makes the check count what the backlog counts.
\item \textbf{The throughput fix agrees with the served share, the old one does not.} Token Passing
  serves all the work at 0.29 tasks per step. Centralised fixed at 0.143 is half of that, as its
  shares of about 0.5 say. At 0.036 it would be an eighth.
\item \textbf{It changes one written claim.} Appendix~L says the decentralised policy and the policy
  with messages trained under fixed storage finish almost nothing, so storage has nothing to change.
  Counted in full they finish 0.030 and 0.113 tasks per step. The policy with messages finishes
  0.035 fewer under congestion-aware storage, worse on all 6 replayed seeds. The centralised result
  stays, no difference ($-0.001$ [$-0.006$, 0.005]).
\item \textbf{The main picture stays.} No learned arm comes near Token Passing, which does 1.5 to 2
  times their work and keeps up on every seed.
\end{itemize}

Token Passing is not affected. It never leaves an order waiting at the end (0.0 in
\texttt{tab:app:backlog:16}), so its rows stay as they are.

\section*{Part 3. Questions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item A waiting line counts as one pick. A line becomes 1.03 to 1.18 tasks on average when its stock
  is spread over shelves, so the count is a little low for those lines. Counting units instead would
  be exact but changes every other count too. One per line, fine? \new{Settled 17 November, option 1, one per line. Counting by order line would need new code and every run played again, for no change in any verdict.}
\item The worked example in green, 800 orders waiting and 10 picks finished, keep it or leave the
  paragraph without numbers?
\item The throughput counts every task finished in the window. This changes the tasks per step of
  every learned run already written, and the Regime~1 finding must be checked again. Agreed?
\item The thesis has no symbol for the warm-up, so $T$ above stays the horizon, while the code divides
  by the measured steps. Add a symbol for the warm-up in the notation table, or say ``per measured
  step'' in words?
\item Order of the work: the code fix on a branch from \texttt{main}, then the fleet 16 replay, then
  fleet 8. Fine?
\end{enumerate}
